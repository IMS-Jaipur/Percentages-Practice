Chapter 1

Fractions 1/1 to 1/20 as percentages

Every percentage shortcut in this chapter rests on knowing these 20 values cold. Hide the values and tap a card to test yourself.

Tap a card to reveal it when hidden.

How to build them instead of memorising blindly

Halving chain

1/2 = 50 → 1/4 = 25 → 1/8 = 12.5 → 1/16 = 6.25

1/3 = 33.33 → 1/6 = 16.67 → 1/12 = 8.33

1/5 = 20 → 1/10 = 10 → 1/20 = 5

Multiples k/n

Multiply the unit value. 3/8 = 3 × 12.5 = 37.5, 5/6 = 5 × 16.67 = 83.33, 2/7 = 28.57.

Or use the complement: 7/8 = 100 − 12.5 = 87.5.

The odd ones

1/7 = 14.28 (digits 142857 repeat for every k/7).

1/9 = 11.11, 1/11 = 9.09 (k/11 = k × 9.09: 18.18, 27.27 …).

1/13 ≈ 7.69, 1/17 ≈ 5.88, 1/19 ≈ 5.26.

Reverse: % → fraction

Spot the unit fraction. 62.5% = 5 × 12.5 = 5/8, 44.44% = 4/9, 18.18% = 2/11.

Chapter 2

Using fractions as multiplying factors

A percentage change is a multiplication. Write the factor as a fraction and the arithmetic usually cancels down to nothing.

The rule

Increase by 1/n ⇒ multiply by (n+1)/n

Decrease by 1/n ⇒ multiply by (n−1)/n

Example

Price ₹840 rises by 12.5% (= 1/8).

New = 840 × 9/8 = 105 × 9 = ₹945. No decimals needed.

Going back to the old value

Flip the fraction. After +14.28%, new = old × 8/7, so old = new × 7/8.

Undo a change

+1/n is undone by −1/(n+1). +25% (×5/4) is undone by ×4/5 = −20%.

−1/n is undone by +1/(n−1). −20% (×4/5) is undone by ×5/4 = +25%.

Chapter 3

Successive percentage change

Three ways to solve the same question. Learn all three, then pick the fastest one for the numbers in front of you.

Example: A price is increased by 20% and then decreased by 25%. Find the net % change.

A. Take the initial value as 100

Best for 3 or more changes, and when you need actual values.

100+20%120−25%90
  1. 100 → 120 → 90
  2. Net change = 90 − 100 = −10%

B. Net % change formula

Best for exactly two changes with round numbers.

  1. Net = a + b + ab/100
  2. = 20 + (−25) + (20 × −25)/100
  3. = −5 − 5 = −10%

C. Multiplying factor

Best when the % values are fraction-friendly (12.5, 16.67, 33.33 …).

  1. +20% = ×6/5, −25% = ×3/4
  2. 6/5 × 3/4 = 9/10
  3. 9/10 = 0.9 ⇒ −10%

Results worth remembering

Same % up and down

Net = −x²/100, in either order. +10% then −10% = −1%.

Order does not matter

Multiplication is commutative, so +20% then −25% equals −25% then +20%.

Successive discounts

20% and 10% ⇒ 20 + 10 − 2 = 28%, never simply 30%.

Unknown second change

Second factor = overall factor ÷ first factor.

Chapter 4

Product of values = new value

When a quantity is a product (P = A × B), a change in A and a change in B act on P exactly like two successive changes. Every method from Chapter 3 works here.

The idea

New P = (A × factor₁) × (B × factor₂) = Old P × factor₁ × factor₂

Where it shows up

Area = L × B · Expenditure = Price × Consumption · Revenue = Price × Units · Distance = Speed × Time · Work = Men × Hours × Days

Product stays the same

The other factor must invert. Price ×5/4 ⇒ consumption ×4/5, a 20% cut.

Squares and cubes

Side +10% ⇒ area (11/10)² = +21%, volume (11/10)³ = +33.1%.

Example: Length +25%, breadth −20%. Area?  5/4 × 4/5 = 1 ⇒ no change. By formula: 25 − 20 − 5 = 0%.
